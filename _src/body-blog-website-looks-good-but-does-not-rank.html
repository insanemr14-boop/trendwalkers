Why a Good-Looking Website Still Does Not Rank

Published 13 November 2026 · Trend Walkers team

A site can win design awards and be invisible in search. Design is judged by people looking at the page. Search engines judge something else, and most of it is not on screen.

1. Search engines cannot see the content

Sites built to load content with JavaScript, or with text baked into images, can look complete to a visitor and nearly empty to a crawler. Check: view the page source and search for a sentence from your page. If it is not there, that is your problem.

2. Pages are not indexed

A leftover "noindex" tag from the staging site, or a blocking rule in robots.txt, will keep an entire site out of search. It is one of the most common faults after a redesign. Check: search Google for site:yourdomain.com and see how many pages come back.

3. Every page targets nothing in particular

A homepage that says "We create digital experiences" tells a search engine nothing about what you sell or where. Each important page needs one clear subject, stated in the title, the main heading and the first paragraph.

4. There is not enough on the page

Minimal design often means minimal text. Three lines and a large photo is not enough for a search engine to understand a service. You do not need long pages. You need pages that answer the questions a buyer actually has: what it is, who it is for, what it costs, how it works.

5. The site is slow on a phone

Large hero videos and uncompressed images look fine on office wifi and stall on mobile data. Check: run the page through Google PageSpeed Insights and look at the mobile score, not the desktop one.

6. Nothing links to it

A new site with no links from other websites has no reputation. Start with what you can earn honestly: supplier and partner listings, local business directories, industry associations, and press about real work.

7. The old URLs were not redirected

If the redesign changed page addresses and nobody set up redirects, the old site's rankings were thrown away. This is recoverable, but the sooner the redirects go in, the more is saved.

What to fix first

Work in this order: indexing, then redirects, then page titles and headings, then speed, then content depth, then links. The first two take hours and can restore traffic within weeks. The last two take months and are where the long-term growth comes from.
