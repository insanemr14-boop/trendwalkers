9 Questions to Ask a Digital Marketing Agency Before You Sign

Published 13 November 2026 · Trend Walkers team

Every agency pitch sounds the same. The differences show up in month four, when it is expensive to leave. These questions surface them before you sign.

1. Who will actually work on my account?

Ask for names. Many agencies pitch with senior people and deliver with juniors. A good answer names the people in the room. A weak answer is "a dedicated team".

2. What would you not do for a business like mine?

An agency that says yes to every channel is selling line items. You want someone who will tell you that a channel is wrong for your business before billing you for it.

3. Can I see the work, not just the report?

You should have access to the content calendar, the ad accounts, the analytics and the drafts. If the answer is that you will receive a monthly PDF, you are being managed, not informed.

4. Who owns the accounts and the assets?

Ad accounts, analytics properties, the website, the domain and the creative files should be in your name. If the agency owns them, leaving means starting again from nothing.

5. What does month one look like, week by week?

A real plan has specifics: audit in week one, fixes in week two, first content in week three. A vague answer about onboarding and strategy usually means there is no plan yet.

6. How do you report things that did not work?

Everything underperforms sometimes. Ask for an example of a campaign that missed and what they changed. An agency that only reports wins is editing.

7. How do you build links and followers?

Bought links, engagement pods and follower packages work until they do not, and then you carry the penalty. Ask directly. Hesitation is an answer.

8. What is the minimum term, and how do I leave?

Ninety days is reasonable: long enough for compounding channels to show signal. Twelve-month lock-ins mostly protect the agency. Check the notice period and what gets handed over.

9. What result should I expect, and by when?

Nobody can promise a ranking or a revenue number. They can tell you which metrics should move first and roughly when. Guarantees of "page one in 30 days" are a reason to walk away.

How to use this list

Send the questions before the call, not during it. The quality of the written answers tells you most of what you need. Then ask for two clients you can speak to, one of them a client who left.
